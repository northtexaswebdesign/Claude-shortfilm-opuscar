# Facts in "Why Do We Sneeze at the Sun?"

Checked with a web search during production (2026-10-25). Paste the sources into the YouTube description.

| Claim in the film | Source | How sure |
|---|---|---|
| It's called the photic sneeze reflex | Standard term; Scientific American — https://scientificamerican.com/article/looking-at-the-sun-can-trigger-a-sneeze/ | Solid |
| Between one in ten and one in three people have it | PBS NewsHour: estimated 10–35% — https://www.pbs.org/newshour/amp/science/does-the-sun-make-you-sneeze ; Swedish donor study ~24% (PMC2821404) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2821404 | Solid as a range |
| Aristotle puzzled over it more than 2,000 years ago | PBS: Aristotle referenced it in the 4th century BC (*Problems*) | Solid |
| A name that spells ACHOO (Autosomal Dominant Compelling Helio-Ophthalmic Outburst) | Collie et al. 1978; PBS, Helix — https://www.helix.com/blog/genetics-dna-achoo-syndrome-allergies (expansions vary slightly between sources) | Solid; the film doesn't spell out the words |
| Normal sneeze: irritant → trigeminal nerve → sneeze centre in the brainstem | Standard physiology | Solid |
| A sneeze carries "by some counts" tens of thousands of droplets | Commonly cited (Jennison 1942 high-speed photos; estimates up to ~40,000) | Fairly sure; hedged |
| Leading theory: optic-nerve signal spills into trigeminal pathways; not proven | Scientific American ("crossed wires… probably responsible"); PBS | Hedged in the film ("leading theory", "aren't completely sure") |
| Usually triggered by a sudden change from dark to bright; usually one or a few sneezes | Standard descriptions (PBS, Discover — https://www.discovermagazine.com/why-does-the-sun-make-me-sneeze-here-s-what-to-know-about-achoo-syndrome-47989) | Solid |
| Runs in families; dominant inheritance | Collie 1978, Peroutka 1984 family studies (summarised at https://dismech.monarchinitiative.org/pages/research/Achoo_Syndrome-cyberian.html) | Solid |
| 2010 genetic study of ~5,000 people found a spot on chromosome 2 | Eriksson et al. 2010 (23andMe), 5,390 participants, rs10427255 on 2q22.3 | Solid; "about five thousand" |
| Safety line: don't stare at the sun | Common sense / eye safety | — |
