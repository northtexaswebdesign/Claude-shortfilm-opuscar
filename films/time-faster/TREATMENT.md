# Why Time Feels Faster as You Get Older — Treatment

Style: Urban Sketch · Pen & Wash (`styles/urban-sketch/STYLE.md`). 1920×1080, 24 fps, ~60 s.
English narration (Kokoro, offline), handwritten captions in the lower margin, `.srt`.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **The flip-book diary.** One sketchbook, one page per year; the pages turn faster and faster as the person ages, until they blur. Clear, but it only *shows* the feeling; it can't explain the two reasons, and a page turn every second becomes noise.
2. **The life ruler (chosen).** One long horizontal page is a life, read left to right as a ruler from age 0. The camera travels along it. Childhood is painted dense with colour (memories); adulthood is an ink-only stretch where the same commute is drawn over itself; at the end colour comes back. The page *is* the argument: the proportion idea is a measurement on the ruler, the memory idea is how much colour each stretch holds.
3. **Five versus fifty.** A split page: a five-year-old's single summer day on the left, a fifty-year-old's whole year on the right, drawn in the same time. Strong contrast, but static, and it gives away the ending in the first shot.

**Why 2:** it is a single journey (the camera moves forward in time like a life does), it carries both explanations in one image system, and it makes the medium's native move — *colour as arrival / the one coloured thing* — the mechanism: **colour = a new memory**. Ink without wash is time that left no mark.

Opening image: a blank cream page with one handwritten date, then a child's kite drawn line by line. Ending: the adult flies a kite (an echo of the opening), and colour floods the routine stretch. Score shape: a jazz waltz that starts full and bright, thins to a single clarinet over a ticking bass in the routine, stops dead, then returns.

## 2. Logline and arc

**Logline:** A life drawn in a sketchbook shows why the years speed up — each new year is a smaller slice of the whole, and routine leaves no colour behind — and how to slow them down again.

- **Setup (0–15 s):** a five-year-old's endless summer in full colour; then a year that flicks past for an adult. The question.
- **Turn (15–41 s):** reason one, proportion (the ruler). Silence. Reason two, the stranger one: the brain measures time in memories, and routine leaves none (the commute traced over itself into one line).
- **Ending (41–60 s):** do something new; colour floods the grey stretch; the page pulls back and the year is long again.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Learn from:** the best of reportage-sketchbook animation and essay explainers: one calm narrator, one visual metaphor sustained through the whole film instead of a new picture per sentence; a quiet emotional landing after the facts.
- **Don't take:** any channel's characters, colour design, mascots, layouts or music; no cosmic space imagery or flat vector look. This is a paper sketchbook, sepia line, wash.

## 6. Cue map

- Jazz waltz, 3/4, 120 bpm → one bar = 1.5 s, 40 bars = 60 s.
- **A (bars 1–6, 0–9 s):** piano comping + walking bass in three + brushes; enters on the first wash bloom (bar 3).
- **B (bars 7–10, 9–15 s):** same groove, brushes double-time feel; calendar flicks on each beat.
- **C (bars 11–17, 15–25.5 s):** bass + piano, lighter; ruler ticks on downbeats.
- **Silence (bar 18, 25.5–27 s).**
- **D (bars 19–23, 27–34.5):** the narrator's voice is the first sound after the silence; bass and piano return softly, the clarinet melody enters on bar 20 (28.5 s) as the clock is drawn.
- **E (bars 24–29, 34.5–43.5):** routine: bass on one note, ticking rim, no piano; pen redraw loops on each bar.
- **F (bars 30–34, 43.5–51):** clarinet alone, then full band returns on the kite's colour bloom (bar 31).
- **G (bars 35–40, 51–60):** full waltz, final cadence on bar 39, ring out.
