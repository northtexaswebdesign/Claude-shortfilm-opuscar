# Why Time Feels Faster as You Get Older — New Yorker Cartoon treatment

Style: the user's New Yorker Cartoon, Ink and Wash prompt (`STYLE.md`). 1920×1080, 24 fps, 60 s.
Same narration as the Pen & Wash version (Kokoro `af_heart`, checked); the narration lines are the hand-lettered captions.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **One panel, one gag, per sentence.** A run of single-panel cartoons, each a deadpan joke that illustrates one line; the caption is the punchline. Natural to the medium, risks feeling like a slideshow.
2. **The museum of you (chosen, merged with 1).** Panels of a life (a beach at five, two birthdays, two cakes) alternate with one recurring set: a small museum inside your head, its gallery walls hung with memories and minded by a sleepy guard. Childhood's wing is crowded; the wing for ages 30–45 is empty; at the end the guard hangs new pictures. A recurring set gives the film a spine and a payoff.
3. **One man, one day.** A single adult across one ordinary day, cut against flashbacks. Too little room for the two explanations in 60 s.

**Why 2:** single-panel gags are what this style is for, and the museum turns the abstract idea ("your brain keeps memories, not a clock") into a place we can return to. The split frame (two drawings side by side) carries every comparison: 38 vs 39, five slices vs fifty, the queue vs the beach.

## 2. Logline and arc

A man who has stopped noticing the years finds out why — each year is a smaller slice of his life, and routine hangs nothing on the walls of memory — and steps out of the queue.
- Setup (0–15): an endless summer at five; then two identical birthdays a year apart.
- Turn (15–46.5): the cake (1/5 vs 1/50); silence; the museum of memories; the queue of a hundred identical mornings; the empty wing.
- Ending (46.5–60): he leaves the queue for the beach; the guard hangs new pictures.

## 3. Benchmark

- Learn: mid-century magazine cartooning — one clear gag per panel, staging readable at a glance, faces that act, washes that stay pale, deadpan captions that undercut the picture.
- Don't take: any cartoonist's characters, drawings, dogs, lettering or captions. Our cast is our own: a kid with a mop of hair, a bald man with a big nose, a museum guard.
